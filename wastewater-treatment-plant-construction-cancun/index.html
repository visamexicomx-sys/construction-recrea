Wastewater Treatment Plants for Cancún Hotels and Developments

Central Cancún has a municipal sewer network operated under the Aguakan concession, but a large part of the city's growth does not: Costa Mujeres and Playa Mujeres, the developments along the road to the airport and Puerto Morelos, the outer supermanzanas and many hotel properties that treat their own water to reuse it. Large towers in Puerto Cancún and the Hotel Zone also face connection limits and pre-treatment requirements. We design and build treatment plants that fit those conditions.

Cancún 2026: compact plant for a boutique hotel or small condo (10–50 m³/day) $900,000–$3.5M MXN; plant for a Hotel Zone resort or tower (50–200 m³/day) $3.5M–$12M MXN; plant for a residential development in Costa Mujeres or the airport corridor (200–1,000 m³/day) $12M–$45M+ MXN.

Sewer, own plant or both?

Whether a Cancún project connects to the network depends on location and the operator's capacity at that point. Inside the urban area, many projects connect but must meet discharge limits for municipal sewer (NOM-002) and install grease and solids pre-treatment. Outside it — Costa Mujeres, the corridor south towards Puerto Morelos, isolated lots — a full treatment plant is usually the only option, and the effluent becomes irrigation water. We confirm feasibility before design so you do not size a plant you do not need, or miss one you do.

Built near the lagoon and the sea

Cancún sits between the Caribbean and the Nichupté lagoon system, which has suffered from wastewater pollution for decades. Authorities are strict about any discharge near it. We build watertight tanks, tested before backfilling, design for high water tables on lagoon and coastal lots, and prefer reuse over discharge wherever possible.
