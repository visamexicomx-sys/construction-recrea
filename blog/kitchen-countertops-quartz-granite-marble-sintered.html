Countertops: Quartz, Granite, Marble or Sintered Stone in a Caribbean House

Countertops look similar in the showroom and behave very differently in a Caribbean villa: sun on the outdoor kitchen, lime juice and tequila at the bar, guests who don't use boards. Prices installed, Quintana Roo, 2026.

Related: Vigueta y Bovedilla vs Solid Slab vs Steel Deck · Concrete Block vs ICF vs Steel Frame vs Concrete Walls in the Riviera Maya · Stone Cladding for Façades in the Riviera Maya

1. Materials compared

MaterialInstalled (MXN/m²)StrengthsWeak points
Granite$4,500–$9,000Hard, heat-resistant, fine outdoors if sealedNeeds sealing; limited modern looks
Quartz (engineered)$7,000–$15,000Non-porous, uniform, low maintenance indoorsYellows and fades under UV: not for outdoor kitchens; can scorch with hot pans
Marble$6,000–$18,000BeautyEtches with lime and acids, stains; high maintenance in rentals
Sintered stone / ultra-compact (porcelain slabs)$12,000–$25,000UV, heat and stain resistant; best for outdoor kitchensEdges chip if hit; needs skilled fabricator
Polished concrete / microcement$4,000–$8,000Seamless, artisanalCracks, needs sealing; stains in rentals

2. What we specify

  • Outdoor kitchens and bars: sintered stone or granite; see outdoor kitchens.
  • Indoor kitchen, rental villa: quartz or sintered stone.
  • Owner's kitchen, marble lovers: honed marble accepting patina, or marble-look sintered stone.
  • Bathrooms: quartz, sintered stone or local stone with sealer.

3. Details that matter

  • Supports for long overhangs and islands.
  • Undermount sinks need precise cut-outs; ask for stainless 316 near the sea.
  • Joint placement on large islands (slab size limits).
  • Shipping and handling of large slabs to islands like Cozumel and Holbox adds cost.
