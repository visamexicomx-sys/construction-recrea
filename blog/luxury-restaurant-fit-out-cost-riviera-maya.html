Luxury Restaurant Fit-Out in the Riviera Maya: Cost per m² and What Drives It

A premium restaurant is two projects in one: a dining room and bar that sells the experience, and a commercial kitchen that decides the budget and the licences. This guide gives the 2026 cost per m² by level, a worked budget for a 200 m² venue, what pushes the price up on the coast, and how long it takes from keys to opening night.

Related services: Premium restaurant & bar fit-out · Restaurant fit-out in Cancún · Restaurant fit-out in Playa del Carmen · Kitchen extraction systems · Stainless steel kitchens

1. Cost per m² by level (2026)

LevelTypical venueDining & bar (MXN/m²)Kitchen package (MXN)
Premium casualCafé, bistro, beach bar, 80–150 m²$18,000–$25,000$1.5M–$2.5M
Fine dining / cocktail barChef-led restaurant, 150–300 m²$25,000–$35,000$2.5M–$4M
Signature / hotel venueRooftop, flagship, hotel restaurant$35,000–$55,000$4M–$6M+

Prices are for the fit-out of a shell or a gutted unit, before kitchen equipment, furniture and VAT. The kitchen package covers the works around the equipment: extraction and make-up air, gas, drainage with grease trap, cold rooms, washable finishes and the electrical capacity.

2. A worked example: 200 m² chef-led restaurant

ItemBasisBudget (MXN)
Dining room and bar, 140 m²$25,000–$35,000 / m²$3.5M–$4.9M
Kitchen package, 60 m²Fine-dining level$2.5M–$4M
Total before equipment and furniture$6M–$8.9M

Kitchen equipment, furniture, tableware and the designer’s fee come on top. A terrace or rooftop adds structure, waterproofing and shade; a beachfront venue adds federal zone rules.

3. What pushes the price up

  • Extraction and smoke: hoods, ducts to the roof, make-up air and, in plazas or towers, filtration so the smell never reaches neighbours.
  • Air conditioning for an open kitchen and a full room at 35 °C outside, plus humidity control so finishes do not sweat.
  • Acoustics: hard luxury finishes are loud; absorbent ceilings and panels keep the room comfortable.
  • The bar: custom joinery, stone, back-bar lighting, ice and glass-washing, draught lines.
  • Rooftops and terraces: structural check, waterproofing, glass balustrades, shade rated for hurricane wind.
  • Imported items: lighting, stone and equipment with long lead times change both price and opening date.

4. Remodel or new fit-out?

Taking over a former restaurant can save the extraction route, the grease trap and the gas line, if they meet current rules and the new menu. A full strip-out is cheaper than fighting a bad layout, though: when the kitchen is in the wrong place, the extraction has no route to the roof or the drains have no fall, start again. We survey the unit before you sign the lease.

5. Licences that run in parallel

  • Land use and operating licence from the municipality (Benito Juárez, Solidaridad, Tulum...).
  • Protección Civil: risk analysis, extinguishers, emergency lighting and exits, reviewed before opening.
  • Health notice and good practices for food handling.
  • Alcohol licence, which depends on the land use and the type of venue.
  • Plaza or building approval of drawings, extraction route and works hours.

6. Timeline

Typically 8–14 weeks of works for a 150–300 m² venue after the design is approved, depending on the kitchen and imported items. Add design, landlord approvals and licences before that. We plan backwards from the opening date.
