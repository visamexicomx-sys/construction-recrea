Hotel Construction Permits in Quintana Roo (2026)

A hotel in Quintana Roo needs more than a building licence. Land use and room density, a federal or state environmental authorisation, the beach concession, civil protection, health and tourism registrations all sit on the path between a lot and an open hotel. Here is the order that works, who issues what, and how long it takes.

Related services: Hotel and commercial construction · Permit management · Zone checker

1. Start with the municipality and the land use

The authority depends on where the lot is: Benito Juárez (Cancún), Isla Mujeres (including Costa Mujeres and Playa Mujeres on the mainland), Puerto Morelos (independent since 2016), Solidaridad (Playa del Carmen), Tulum, Cozumel, Bacalar, Othón P. Blanco (Chetumal, Mahahual) or Lázaro Cárdenas (Holbox). Check the cadastral record before you design: older documents sometimes still name the former municipality.

The land-use certificate (uso de suelo) and the urban development programme decide whether hotel use is allowed and, crucially, the room density — rooms per hectare — plus height, coverage and parking. The key count in your business plan must come from this document, not from the architect’s first sketch.

2. The environmental authorisation: federal or state

Which authority reviews the environmental impact depends on the site:

  • SEMARNAT (federal) — coastal ecosystems, dunes, mangroves (NOM-022), federal maritime zone, cenotes and most beachfront hotels. This is a Manifestación de Impacto Ambiental (MIA) and is often the critical path: plan four to eight months or more.
  • SEMA (Quintana Roo state) — many inland projects, for example in Tulum’s regions and jungle developments, where the authorisation also covers vegetation removal and flora rescue.
  • CONANP — inside protected natural areas such as Yum Balam (Holbox) or Sian Ka’an, under the area’s management programme.

The environmental resolution comes with conditions — on runoff, lighting, discharge, construction methods and schedule — that become part of the construction contract.

3. Beachfront: ZOFEMAT and turtle-season rules

The first 20 metres from the high-tide line are federal maritime zone. Using them for loungers, decks, shade or a beach club needs a separate ZOFEMAT concession with an annual fee; rooms and kitchens go behind the line. On nesting beaches, exterior lighting facing the sea must be shielded, low and warm during turtle season.

4. Cancún Hotel Zone and master-planned developments

In the Cancún Hotel Zone the FONATUR plan fixes hotel density and uses, with federal frontage on both the sea and the Nichupté lagoon. In resort developments such as Mayakoba, Puerto Cancún, Playa Mujeres or Punta Maroma, the developer’s design committee reviews the project before the municipality. File both in parallel so neither waits for the other.

5. Construction licence, utilities and Protección Civil

  • Construction licence with a registered DRO (responsible building official), structural and installation projects.
  • Water and drainage feasibility (CAPA) — or an on-site treatment plant where there is no network.
  • CFE feasibility and, for most hotels, a transformer or a medium-voltage connection.
  • Protección Civil — risk analysis, emergency plan, fire and evacuation design, reviewed again before opening.

6. Opening: health, operation and tourism

  • COFEPRIS sanitary notice or licence for restaurants, bars, spas and pools.
  • Operating licence (licencia de funcionamiento) from the municipality.
  • Registro Nacional de Turismo registration for the lodging business.
  • Final Protección Civil inspection and the notice of completion of the works.

Foreign owners

Within 50 km of the coast, foreigners hold land through a bank trust (fideicomiso) or a Mexican company. For an operating hotel, a Mexican company is usually the vehicle, because it also signs the operating licence, tourism registration, payroll and contracts. Coordinate the corporate structure with your notary and accountant before the licence is filed in its name.
