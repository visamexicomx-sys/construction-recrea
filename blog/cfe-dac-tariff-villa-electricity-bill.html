CFE's DAC Tariff: Why Villa Bills Jump and How to Bring Them Down

Owners are often surprised by a bill several times larger than expected. The reason is usually that the house has crossed into DAC (Doméstica de Alto Consumo), CFE's unsubsidised residential tariff. Avoiding it is a design decision.

Related: Roof Insulation and Cool Roofs in the Riviera Maya · Water Heaters for a House in the Riviera Maya · LP Gas Stationary Tank for a House or Restaurant in Mexico · Water Pressure in a Villa · Pool Heating in the Riviera Maya · Internet in a Villa in the Riviera Maya

1. How it works

Residential tariffs in hot regions (1B to 1F, depending on the town's summer temperatures) are subsidised up to a monthly consumption limit. If your 12-month moving average exceeds the limit for your tariff, CFE moves you to DAC, where every kWh costs several times more plus a fixed charge. You only return after the average drops below the limit.

4. Commercial and rental properties

Hotels, restaurants and many rental businesses are on commercial tariffs with demand charges; a transformer and medium-voltage tariff may be cheaper above a certain size. See utility connections and solar installation cost.
