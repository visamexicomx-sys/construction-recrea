Renovating a Condo: The HOA Rules That Decide Your Schedule

In a condo the administration, not the municipality, is usually what slows a renovation. Work hours, elevator use and approvals can turn a 6-week job into 12. This is what most buildings in Cancún, Playa del Carmen and Tulum ask for, and how to plan for it.

Related: Do You Need a Permit to Renovate in Quintana Roo?

1. Typical requirements

RequirementTypical
Written request with scope and plans1–3 weeks for approval; structural or façade changes may need assembly
Damage depositMXN 5,000–50,000, refunded after inspection
Work hoursOften Mon–Fri 8:00–17:00/18:00, Sat mornings; noisy work restricted further; no Sundays
Worker registrationID list, sometimes IMSS proof, badges
Contractor insuranceCivil liability policy
Elevator and corridor protectionMandatory; booked service elevator
Debris removalBagged, at set times, contractor's own container

2. What you usually can't touch

  • Structure: columns, beams, slabs, load-bearing walls.
  • Façade, balcony railings, window colour and profile.
  • Common risers: drainage, water, electrical and gas shafts.
  • A/C condenser locations outside the assigned area.
  • Waterproofing of terraces that cover units below without approval.

3. How it changes cost and time

Restricted hours and elevator slots lower productivity 20–35% compared with a house. On a full apartment renovation that is typically 2–4 extra weeks. We include it in the programme and price from the start; see condo renovation and condo fit-out cost.

4. Checklist before starting

  • Read the bylaws and the building's renovation manual.
  • Get written approval and keep it on site.
  • Check if a municipal notice or licence is also needed (see renovation permits).
  • Photograph common areas before work to protect the deposit.
  • Agree on a single contact between contractor and administration.
