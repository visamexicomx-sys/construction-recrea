Water Heaters: Solar, Gas or Heat Pump for a Caribbean House

Hot water is a small line in the budget and a big source of complaints in rentals: not enough for four showers, scale-clogged heaters after two years, or gas deliveries nobody arranged. This is how we choose and size it.

1. Options (2026)

TypeInstalled cost (MXN)Running costNotes
Solar thermosiphon 150–300 L (evacuated tubes)$9,000–$28,000Almost zeroNeeds roof space; add backup
LP gas instant (tankless)$8,000–$25,000MediumEndless hot water; needs gas tank and venting
LP gas storage tank$6,000–$15,000MediumSimple; standby losses
Electric storage tank$5,000–$14,000HighCan push villas into DAC tariff
Heat-pump water heater$35,000–$80,000Low3–4x more efficient than electric; works well in hot climate

2. Our usual recommendation

  • Houses and villas: solar as primary + heat pump or instant gas as backup.
  • Rental villas: size for all bedrooms showering in the same hour.
  • Condos: heat pump or electric where gas is not allowed.

3. Hard water

Water on the peninsula is very hard (limestone). Without a softener, heaters, solar tubes and shower heads scale up in 1–3 years. A whole-house softener costs MXN 18,000–55,000 and protects every fixture. See plumbing and water systems.
