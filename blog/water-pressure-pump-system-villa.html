Water Pressure in a Villa: Getting Rain-Shower Pressure on Every Floor

Municipal pressure in Cancún, Playa del Carmen and Tulum is low and intermittent, so almost every house stores water in a cistern. What you put after the cistern decides whether a rain shower on the second floor works. These are the three systems and their costs.

1. Systems compared

SystemPressureInstalled cost (MXN)Best for
Cistern + pump to rooftop tank (tinaco), gravityLow, depends on height$8,000–$20,000Small one-storey houses
Hydropneumatic tank + pumpGood, but fluctuates$15,000–$40,000Houses with 2–3 bathrooms
Variable-speed (VFD) booster pumpConstant 40–60 psi$35,000–$110,000Villas, rain showers, rentals
Duplex VFD system with standby pumpConstant + redundancy$90,000–$250,000Boutique hotels, condos

2. Cistern sizing

Plan at least 3–5 days of consumption: about 250–300 L per person per day in a villa, more with garden irrigation. A 4-bedroom villa usually needs 10–20 m³. See water storage sizing and cisterns.

3. Details that avoid problems

  • Filter and softener after the pump to protect fixtures from hard-water scale.
  • Pump room ventilated, drained and accessible.
  • Pressure-reducing valve where pressure would exceed 60–70 psi.
  • Backup power for the pump, or the house has no water in a blackout.

4. Running cost

A VFD booster uses energy only to match demand; in a villa it typically costs less to run than an on-off pump cycling a small pressure tank.
