Termites and Wood: Building So They Don't Win

Subterranean and drywood termites are active all year in Tulum, Playa del Carmen, Bacalar and Cancún. Jungle lots, wooden ceilings, palapas and carpentry are their favourite targets. Prevention during construction costs a fraction of later treatment.

Related: Salt-Air Corrosion · Mold in Tropical Houses · Sargassum and Your Beachfront House · Concrete Spalling and Exposed Rebar in Coastal Buildings

1. The two kinds

TypeHow it attacksTypical signs
Subterranean termitesFrom the soil through cracks, joints and pipes; mud tubesTubes on walls, hollow door frames
Drywood termitesFly in and live inside wood; no soil contactSmall pellets under furniture and beams

2. During construction

MeasureCost (MXN, 2026)
Soil pre-treatment under the slab and around foundations$35–$70 per m² of footprint
Physical barrier at pipe penetrations$3,000–$8,000 per house
Treated or naturally durable wood only (no untreated pine)Included in spec
Wood not in contact with soil; concrete or steel basesDetailing
Factory-treated carpentry (closets, doors)+8–15% on carpentry

3. Woods that last here

Tropical hardwoods such as chicozapote, jabín, tzalam and ipe resist far better than pine or MDF. Tzalam is the usual choice for doors and ceilings; it still needs a finish and inspection. For palapas, the zacate and poles need periodic treatment; see palapa construction and tropical hardwoods.

4. After handover

  • Annual inspection (MXN 1,500–3,500).
  • Spot treatment of drywood colonies when found.
  • Re-treatment of soil barrier every 5–8 years depending on product.
  • Keep firewood, boxes and garden debris away from walls.
