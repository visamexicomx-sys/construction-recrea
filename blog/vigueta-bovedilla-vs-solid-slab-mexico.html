Which Slab for a House in Mexico: Vigueta y Bovedilla, Solid, Steel Deck or Waffle

The slab is the single largest structural item in a Mexican house. Choosing the system changes cost, spans, ceiling height, insulation and how it behaves near the sea. These are the options we use in the Riviera Maya and their 2026 prices.

1. Systems compared

SystemTypical spanCost (MXN/m², 2026)Notes
Vigueta y bovedilla (precast joists + EPS or concrete blocks)Up to 6–7 m$900–$1,400Fast, light; EPS blocks add insulation
Solid reinforced slab (losa maciza)4–6 m$1,300–$1,900Robust, simple; best for first line and cantilevers
Steel deck (losacero) on steel beamsPer beam spacing$1,100–$1,700Fast; protect from corrosion near the sea
Waffle / ribbed slab with voids (reticular)8–12 m$1,500–$2,300Large open spaces, thicker slab
Post-tensioned slab8–14 m$1,800–$2,800Towers, parking, long cantilevers; specialist crew

Structural slab only, without finishes or waterproofing.

2. When to use each

  • Vigueta y bovedilla: economical houses and inland villas with regular spans.
  • Solid slab: beachfront, wet areas, roofs with pools or heavy planters, cantilevered terraces.
  • Waffle: open-plan living rooms and commercial spaces without intermediate columns.
  • Steel deck: mezzanines, extensions over existing structures, fast commercial work.
  • Post-tensioned: apartment towers and parking structures.

3. Coastal and hurricane notes

Near the sea, thin cover on precast joists and exposed steel deck are the weak points; we specify solid slabs or extra cover and coatings in the first 300 m (see salt-air materials). All systems resist hurricanes if the roof is properly tied to beams and walls; see hurricane-proof construction.

4. Thermal effect

EPS bovedilla gives some insulation from below; a solid slab needs insulation on top. Either way, finish with a reflective roof; see roof insulation.
