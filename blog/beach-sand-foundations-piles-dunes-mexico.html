Foundations on Beach Sand: Mats, Piles, Dunes and Mangroves

Away from the limestone ridge, beachfront lots in Tulum, Puerto Morelos, Mahahual and Costa Mujeres sit on loose sand with a shallow, salty water table. The foundation choice, and what you leave untouched in front of the house, decides how it behaves in the next hurricane.

Related: Stormwater and Flooding on a Lot in the Riviera Maya · Water Filtration for a House in the Riviera Maya

1. What the soil study must tell you

  • Depth of loose sand and of competent rock or cemented layer.
  • Water table depth and salinity (affects concrete specification).
  • Bearing capacity and expected settlement.
  • Erosion history of the beach.

See soil study.

3. Dunes and the beach

  • Build behind the dune, don't level it: the dune is the first hurricane defence.
  • Elevated walkways over the dune instead of cutting paths.
  • Keep the federal strip (ZOFEMAT) free of permanent works.
  • Turtle-friendly lighting on the beach façade.

4. Mangroves

Mangroves are strictly protected in Mexico; removing or filling them is generally prohibited, and lots that contain mangrove have reduced buildable area and federal environmental review. See environmental impact assessment.

5. Concrete near salt water

Sulphate- and chloride-resistant concrete, higher cover and waterproofing of buried elements; see concrete types.
