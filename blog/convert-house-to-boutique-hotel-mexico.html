Converting a House into a Boutique Hotel or Hostel: Rules and Cost

Turning a large house into a guesthouse, boutique hotel or hostel is one of the fastest ways into hospitality in Tulum and Playa del Carmen. It is not a decoration project: the use changes, and with it the rules on density, fire safety, exits and wastewater.

Related: Pre-Purchase Home Inspection in Mexico

1. Check if the use is allowed

  • Land-use certificate: lodging (hospedaje) must be permitted in that zone.
  • Density in rooms per hectare or per lot under the urban plan.
  • Parking requirements per room.
  • Condo or HOA bylaws if the house is in a gated community (often prohibit hotels).

See Playa del Carmen zoning.

2. What changes in the building

ItemTypical requirement
Fire safetyDetectors, extinguishers, signage, emergency lighting; hydrants above a size
Exits and stairsWidth, protected routes, doors opening outward
AccessibilityAt least some accessible rooms and routes in new hotels
WastewaterTreatment plant instead of septic tank as capacity grows
KitchenExtraction, grease trap and gas installation if serving food
AcousticsSeparation between rooms

See fire system and civil protection and grease traps.

3. Cost per room (2026)

ScopeMXN per room
Light conversion (existing en-suite rooms, finishes, safety)$250,000–$450,000
Medium (new bathrooms, MEP, partitions)$450,000–$700,000
Heavy (extension, structure, new floors)$700,000–$1,200,000+

Excludes FF&E and pre-opening; see boutique hotel construction.

4. Permits and timeline

  • Use change / land-use approval: 1–4 months.
  • Construction or renovation licence with DRO.
  • Civil protection approval and operating licence.
  • Tourism registration (RNT and state registry), lodging tax registration.
  • Total: typically 4–9 months including works.
