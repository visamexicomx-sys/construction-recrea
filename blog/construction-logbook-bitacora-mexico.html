Bitácora de Obra: The Logbook That Decides Disputes

Every licensed build in Quintana Roo keeps a bitácora de obra: a bound, numbered logbook on site signed by the DRO (responsible director), the builder and the supervisor. Most foreign owners never see it. They should: in a dispute, it is the first document anyone reads.

Related: Construction Payment Schedule in Mexico · Obra Negra, Obra Gris, Obra Blanca and Turnkey · IVA · Construction Handover and Punch List in Mexico · Change Orders and Cost Overruns on a Build in Mexico

1. What it is

The municipal construction regulations require a logbook from the start of works to completion. The DRO opens it, records visits and instructions, and closes it at the end; it supports the completion notice (aviso de terminación).

2. What must go in it

  • Start date, licence number, responsible parties.
  • DRO and co-responsible visits (structure, installations) with observations.
  • Inspections before pouring concrete: rebar, formwork, embedded pipes.
  • Concrete test results and material approvals.
  • Instructions, changes and their approval by the owner.
  • Incidents: accidents, stoppages, inspections by authorities.
  • Completion and close-out.

3. Why it protects the owner

SituationWhat the logbook shows
Crack or leak after handoverWhether the inspection before pouring was done and signed
Builder claims extra costsWhether the change was ordered and approved in writing
Delay disputeStoppages, weather, late owner decisions
Municipal inspection or clausuraThat the works followed the licence and DRO supervision

4. Following it from abroad

Ask for a photo of each new page every week together with the progress report. On our projects the logbook entries are mirrored in the client portal with photos, so remote owners see the same record as the site.

5. Red flags

  • No logbook on site, or it is "at the office".
  • Pages filled weeks later in the same pen.
  • No DRO visits recorded before slab pours.
  • Changes with no owner signature.
