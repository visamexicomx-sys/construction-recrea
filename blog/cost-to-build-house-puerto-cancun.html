How Much Does It Cost to Build in Puerto Cancún? (2026)

A 350 m² villa in Puerto Cancún costs roughly $11.2M–$15.75M MXN ($622k–$875k USD) turnkey in 2026. The address sets the finish level, but the line that surprises most owners sits underground: much of this master plan was built on filled lagoon-edge land, and the foundation is where the budget is won or lost.

Related service: House construction in Puerto Cancún

What makes Puerto Cancún different to build in

Puerto Cancún is a master-planned community between downtown and the Hotel Zone: a golf course, a marina with navigable channels, residential towers and a limited number of single-family lots on the golf and canal sections. Three things shape every villa budget here.

  • Ground conditions. Large parts of the development sit on engineered fill over former mangrove and lagoon margin, with the water table often within one to two metres. A geotechnical study with boreholes is not optional; depending on the result the structure goes on a reinforced mat, on deep footings or on piles. The difference between those options on a 350 m² house is easily $600,000–$1.8M MXN.
  • Master-plan architectural review. The developer’s guidelines govern height, setbacks, roof form, facade palette, landscaping and fencing, and construction is policed: registered crews, set working hours, delivery windows and a deposit against damage to common areas.
  • Canal and marina frontage. A canal lot adds a seawall or bulkhead inspection, a private dock and lift if wanted, and the environmental paperwork that comes with work at the waterline.

Permits run through the municipality of Benito Juárez — land-use compatibility, construction licence and a Director Responsable de Obra — with the master plan’s approval required before the municipal file is submitted.

Lines usually priced separately:

  • Geotechnical study with boreholes: $45,000–$110,000 MXN
  • Piles or deep foundation upgrade, if the study calls for it: $600,000–$1.8M
  • Infinity pool 40–60 m² with equipment room: $900,000–$2.2M
  • Dock, lift and seawall work on a canal lot: $450,000–$1.5M
  • Full home automation, security and networking: $350,000–$1.2M

Programme and where projects slip

Allow 4–6 months for design, the master-plan review and the municipal licence, then 12–18 months of construction for a 300–450 m² villa. The usual slippages are predictable:

  • Dewatering. With a high water table, excavation for cisterns, lift pits and pool shells needs pumping and a dry-season window. Plan the deep work for November to May.
  • Review cycles. Each round of comments from the architectural committee costs two to four weeks. Submit a complete package once, at schematic stage.
  • Imported finishes. Large-format stone, European hardware and glazing systems run 10–16 weeks; order them against the structural programme, not at the end.

Insist on a fixed-price contract built on a line-item budget, milestone payments against verified progress, and weekly dated photo reports. On a project this size, the change-order process should be written down before the first pile is driven.
