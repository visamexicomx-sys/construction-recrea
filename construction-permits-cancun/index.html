How the process changes depending on where the lot sits in Cancún

Talking about permits in Cancún as a single process is misleading: within the same municipality of Benito Juárez the file changes substantially with location. In established urban areas — Palmaris, Cumbres, Villa Magna — the municipal licence is essentially the whole procedure. In gated estates a design committee ruling is added, and it is worth obtaining before the municipal file. In the Hotel Zone the FONATUR planning legacy and federal ZOFEMAT stack on top, and frequently SEMARNAT as well where dune or coastal vegetation is involved. And to the south, near Puerto Morelos, even the competent municipality can change.

The most expensive mistake: designing before you hold the land-use certificate

The land-use certificate is not a procedural box to tick; it is the brief. It fixes COS, CUS, storey count, setbacks and permitted use. Commissioning a concept design before holding it is the most common cause of a full redesign in this city, because the real figures are usually more restrictive than owners assume. The order that works is: land-use certificate and alignment first; soil investigation next; technical design signed by a DRO; a documented drainage solution; and only then the licence application. At the end, completion certificate and cadastral update — the step most often forgotten, and the one that gets paid for on resale.

The permit file in Cancún: what actually goes in

There is no single procedure for the Riviera Maya. In Cancún the competent authority is the Benito Juárez municipality, the environmental regime is municipal; FONATUR in the hotel zone, and that decides which documents enter the file and in what order.

Inland, away from the federal strip, the file is entirely municipal and no environmental authority takes part. That makes Cancún the fastest place in the region to start building: with the land-use certificate in hand and the DRO appointed, the licence resolves without waiting on a third party.

The document that decides it in Cancún

The land-use certificate issued per supermanzana. It is not a formality: it fixes COS, CUS, storey count and the restrictions on that specific lot, and it is what tells you whether the programme fits before you commission a full set of drawings. It is requested with the cadastral key, and the municipal answer normally arrives in two to three weeks. Everything else in the file — licence, DRO, utility feasibility — is built on that sheet. That is why the right order in Cancún is certificate, concept, construction drawings, licence; reversing it is what turns a two-month process into a five-month one.

In Cancún it is not a DRO, it is a Perito Responsable de Obra

Same role, different name, and it is worth knowing before you call the counter. The Solidaridad code, which governs Playa del Carmen, speaks of a Director Responsable de Obra (DRO). The Benito Juárez municipality, which is Cancún, registers and publishes its professionals as Perito Responsable de Obra (P.R.O.), alongside the Corresponsable de Estructura and Corresponsable de Instalaciones roles for projects that require them by size or use.

In practice the function is identical: they sign the project, answer legally for code compliance and keep the site logbook. What changes is the register: a professional listed in Solidaridad is not automatically authorised in Benito Juárez, and vice versa. That is why a file assembled in Playa del Carmen cannot be filed as-is in Cancún.

We work with a professional listed on the register that matches the municipality, and bring in the co-responsible specialists when the project calls for them. In a Cancún filing, that registration is the first thing the town hall checks.
