Wastewater Treatment Plants for Playa del Carmen and Solidaridad

Playa del Carmen has grown faster than its sewer. The centre and the older colonias connect to the network, but the coastal corridor north towards Punta Bete and Punta Maroma, the resorts and residences around Mayakoba, the road south towards Puerto Aventuras and many new subdivisions on the west side depend on their own treatment. Under all of it lies the cave and cenote system that makes this coast unique. We design and build plants that protect it.

Playa del Carmen 2026: compact plant for a boutique hotel or condo (10–50 m³/day) $850,000–$3.2M MXN; plant for a resort or tower (50–200 m³/day) $3.2M–$11M MXN; plant for a development or resort complex (200–1,000 m³/day) $11M–$42M+ MXN.

Above the caves

The land around Playa del Carmen sits over one of the longest underwater cave systems in the world, connected to cenotes and the sea. Any untreated or poorly treated discharge travels fast and far. That is why we build watertight, tested tanks, avoid absorption pits for raw sewage, prefer reuse for irrigation over discharge, and design final disposal with your environmental consultant so the plant passes inspection and protects the aquifer.

Sizing for a seasonal town

Occupancy in Playa swings between low season and peaks at Christmas, Easter and summer holidays. We size for the busiest week, add equalisation for check-out peaks, and choose technologies such as MBBR that cope with load changes, so the plant does not fail exactly when the hotel or condo is full.
