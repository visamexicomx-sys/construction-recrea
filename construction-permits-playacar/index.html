The permit file in Playacar: what actually goes in

There is no single procedure for the Riviera Maya. In Playacar the competent authority is the Solidaridad municipality, the environmental regime is municipal; mature trees protected by the development, and that decides which documents enter the file and in what order.

Salinity rises noticeably in Phase I, next to the sea. At that distance 304 stainless pits within a couple of years; hardware, window frames and anchors have to be specified in 316 from the design stage rather than corrected later.

The file for Playacar, in order

  1. Land-use certificate (Solidaridad municipality)
  2. Construction licence with a registered DRO (responsible building official)
  3. Water and drainage feasibility and contract (CAPA/CONAGUA as applicable)
  4. CFE electrical feasibility and service connection
  5. Sign-off from the development’s design committee or management
  6. Notice of completion and certificate of occupancy

What shapes the process in Playacar

The ground is compact limestone and the water table is deep, which weighs on the soil study that accompanies the licence application. Access is gated access with construction hours and the development regime is per-phase rulebook and design committee.

  • Municipal process only: the town hall sets the calendar, not an environmental authority. This is the fastest scenario in the region.
  • A deep water table allows conventional foundations and, where the study supports it, discharge to an absorption well rather than a treatment plant.
  • Moderate salt exposure: 316 stainless outdoors, 304 acceptable indoors.
  • Design committee or community management: the project is approved twice — committee first, municipality second. Add 4–8 weeks and one round of facade changes.
  • Gated access with working hours and a bond: daily output drops and the schedule stretches versus an open lot.

Realistic timeline in Playacar

With a complete, well-assembled file, count on 4 to 10 weeks: the whole file is municipal, with no environmental authority in between.

We file with the committee first, with the tree inventory and the palette already settled, and only go to the municipality once we hold their ruling. That is the route that avoids the two or three extra rounds projects here usually suffer.

The document that decides it in Playacar

The design committee's ruling, together with the lot's tree inventory. The phase sets the rule and the trees set the position: a couple of protected specimens can move the whole house within the plot. The committee reviews setbacks, height, roofing, window systems and colour palette piece by piece, and any later change restarts the review. That is why the construction set is developed after the ruling rather than before, and why the architect works from the correct phase's rulebook from the very first sketch.

Playacar compared with Corasol

Both pass through a committee before reaching the Solidaridad municipality, but Playacar adds two variables Corasol does not have: the lot’s phase, which changes setbacks and heights, and proximity to the sea in Phase I, where salinity forces 316 stainless to be specified from the design stage. Corasol is more uniform and more predictable. If you are comparing the two, compare hardware and maintenance cost as well, not just land price per square metre. And check the phase before anything else: a Phase I lot and a Phase II lot of identical size can carry different height limits, different setbacks and a different corrosion specification.
