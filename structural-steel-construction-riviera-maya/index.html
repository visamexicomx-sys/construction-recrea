Structural Steel Fabrication and Erection in the Riviera Maya

Steel builds faster, spans further and weighs less than concrete, which matters on rock, on sand and on tight programmes. But on the Caribbean coast it only works if it is designed for hurricane wind and protected against salt air. We engineer, fabricate in our own workshop and erect steel frames for commercial buildings, hotels, warehouses, rooftop additions and villas, from shop drawings to the last bolt.

Quick answer: In 2026 structural steel supplied, fabricated, protected and erected costs about $55–$95 MXN per kg, plus $12–$22/kg for hot-dip galvanizing. A portal-frame building shell with metal roof runs $4,500–$8,500 MXN/m², or $7,000–$13,000 MXN/m² with insulated panels.

Steel or concrete on this coast?

Concrete is the default in Quintana Roo, and for many houses it remains the right choice. Steel wins when you need long spans without columns (warehouses, gyms, showrooms, lobbies), speed (a frame goes up in days once fabricated), light weight (adding floors on an existing building, poor soil) or a slim architectural look. The trade-off is corrosion: unprotected steel near the sea rusts in a few seasons. That is why every project starts with the exposure category and a coating specification, not with the price per kilo.

Designed for hurricanes

  • Wind loads calculated with the CFE 2020 wind design manual for the site, height and exposure, including uplift on roofs and canopies.
  • Connections detailed for load reversal, because hurricane wind pulls as well as pushes.
  • Anchorage to foundations designed for uplift, with anchor bolts cast using templates.
  • Cladding and roofing fixed to the same standard as the frame, since most storm damage starts at the envelope.
