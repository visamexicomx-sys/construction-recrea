Luxury Villas in the Riviera Maya

Where to build and what changes between municipalities — design and construction turnkey

The question is not what a square metre costs. It is which municipality the lot sits in, because that decides the permit calendar, the foundation type and the material specification. A lot 200 metres from the sea and one 20 kilometres inland are designed, permitted and priced differently.

Five municipalities, five calendars

The corridor is not one jurisdiction. Benito Juárez (Cancún) and Solidaridad (Playa del Carmen) inland resolve the file entirely at municipal level: four to ten weeks with a complete package. Tulum adds the state environmental authorisation to practically every project, which turns the process into three to six months. Puerto Morelos has been its own municipality since 2016, with its own code and timelines and the Reef National Park setting the environmental pace. Cozumel and Isla Mujeres add a marine park and, above all, ferry logistics.

On the coastal strip of any of the five, SEMARNAT joins for environmental impact and ZOFEMAT for the beach concession, and the calendar moves to six or twelve months. That fact is worth more than the price difference between two lots, and it almost never appears in the listing.

The ground changes the foundation, not the finish

Inland, in Cancún and Playa del Carmen, the ground is compact limestone with a deep water table: conventional foundations and, where the study supports it, an absorption well rather than a treatment plant. It is the cheapest and fastest scenario on the corridor.

In the Tulum municipality the ground is karst under low jungle: voids are invisible from the surface and verification comes before the foundation is priced. On the coastal strip — from Puerto Morelos down to Tulum — the water sits very high and the subsoil is sand and dune over limestone: compacted fill platform, raft foundation, pool anchored against uplift, basement ruled out.

You can compare all 41 zones of the corridor in the zone checker: municipality, environmental regime, ground, water table, salt exposure and access, with a complexity rating derived from those facts.

Distance to the sea decides the specification

This is not a matter of taste. A few hundred metres from the sea, 304 stainless pits within two years: hardware, window systems, anchors and metalwork go in 316, and concrete cover rises above the code minimum. Inland, 304 holds and the premium is not justified.

The same applies to the roof and the wet rooms. A flat slab in the tropics is a system — falls, parapets, coving, outlets and a flood test before the finish — and a leak at three years destroys everything below it. That is where we most often see a saving that ends up costing double.

What each area gives you

Playacar, Corasol, Mayakoba, Selvamar: communities with a design committee. The project is approved twice and mature trees drive the footprint, but services are in the street and the municipal process is short.

Aldea Zamá, Selvazama, Tulum Country Club: master plan with capped density, a committee and the state SEMA. Slower, with clear design rules from the outset.

Puerto Aventuras, Puerto Cancún: marinas with their own management, registered access and a separate concession for any work at the water’s edge.

Tankah, Bahía Solimán, Punta Bete, Xpu-Há: low-density seafront, frequently off-grid. Treatment plant, own generation and water storage are first-budget line items, not extras.

La Veleta and the Tulum regions: the lowest entry price on the corridor, with CFE and drainage feasibility resolved lot by lot. It is where a price quoted per square metre breaks most often.

How we work across the corridor

Design and construction under one contract, with a fixed price, a fixed programme and payments tied to progress. Changes are documented with a price before they are executed. Weekly reporting with photographs, because most of our clients do not live here.

We have worked with foreign owners since 2008: fideicomiso, payment from abroad, video handover and a power of attorney for a representative when they cannot travel. In-house joinery and metalwork, so kitchen, wardrobes, doors and window systems do not depend on a third party’s calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Where in the Riviera Maya should I build a villa?

It depends on what matters most to you. If it is the calendar, inland Playa del Carmen or Cancún: municipal file, four to ten weeks. If it is the seafront, count six to twelve months of federal process. If it is entry price, the Tulum regions — verifying service feasibility before you buy.

How long do permits take by municipality?

Purely municipal, four to ten weeks. With the state SEMA — practically the whole Tulum municipality — three to six months. With SEMARNAT and ZOFEMAT on the coastal strip, six to twelve. Those are timelines for a complete, well-assembled file.

Can you build on a lot with no drainage or CFE?

Yes, and across much of the corridor it is the normal case. Treatment plant, water storage and own generation or solar with backup. What is not viable is discovering it mid-build: we verify feasibility before pricing.

Do you build across the whole Riviera Maya?

Yes: Cancún, Puerto Morelos, Playa del Carmen, Puerto Aventuras, Akumal, Tulum, Cozumel, Isla Mujeres and Bacalar. What changes between them is the competent authority, the ground and the logistics — not how we work.

Can you supervise if I live abroad?

Yes, that is how most of our clients work. Weekly photo reporting, video calls at milestones, payment by transfer and a power of attorney for a representative when something has to be signed.
