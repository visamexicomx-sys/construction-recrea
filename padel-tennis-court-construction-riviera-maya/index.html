Padel and Tennis Court Construction in the Riviera Maya

A padel court is a precision slab with a glass box on top: if the concrete is off by a few millimetres, the ball bounces wrong and the glass cracks. We build padel and tennis courts for villas, hotels, clubs and gated communities as one package: soil study, slab and drainage, certified court structure, artificial grass or acrylic surface, LED lighting and, when the sun demands it, a steel canopy.

Quick answer: On the Riviera Maya in 2026, a complete outdoor padel court costs roughly $660,000–$1,170,000 MXN: $480,000–$850,000 for the installed court and $180,000–$320,000 for the slab and drainage. A canopy adds $900,000–$1,800,000 MXN.

Why courts fail on this coast, and how we build them to last

Three things ruin courts in the Riviera Maya: slabs that settle or crack on poorly compacted fill, corrosion of untreated steel within two seasons of salt air, and standing water after downpours. We start with a soil study, compact the base in tested layers, pour a slab with controlled flatness (±3 mm under a 3 m straightedge) and specify galvanised, powder-coated structures with stainless fixings. The court installer works on our slab, under our schedule, so nobody blames anybody else.

Padel or tennis, open or covered

  • Space: a padel court needs about 11 × 21 m with clearances; a tennis court about 18 × 36 m. Two padel courts fit where one tennis court would go.
  • Surface: artificial grass with silica sand for padel; acrylic hard court for tennis, which suits the heat better than clay.
  • Cover: a canopy roughly doubles the investment but adds the midday hours and the rainy season, which is what makes a hotel or club court pay.
  • Permits: a court is a structure: licence, setbacks and, in gated communities, the HOA's rules on lighting and noise.
