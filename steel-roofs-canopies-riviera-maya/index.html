Steel Roofs, Canopies and Arched Roofs Built for Hurricanes

Roofs and canopies are the structures that fail first in a hurricane: large, light surfaces that the wind tries to lift. We design and build steel roof structures, self-supporting arched roofs (arcotechos), parking and drop-off canopies, gas-station canopies and roofs for sports courts, schools and terraces with the uplift calculated, the anchorage engineered and the sheeting fixed to resist it.

Quick answer: In 2026 a self-supporting arched roof costs about $1,100–$2,000 MXN/m² installed, depending on span, sheet gauge and edge beams. Columns or walls to support it and foundations are priced with the project.
2026 prices installed: self-supporting arched roof (arcotecho) $1,100–$2,000 MXN/m²; steel-frame roof with metal sheeting $1,800–$3,500 MXN/m²; parking or drop-off canopy (solar-ready) $3,000–$5,500 MXN/m²; architectural canopy with insulated panel or polycarbonate $4,500–$9,000 MXN/m². Why roofs fail and how we prevent it

In a storm the wind does not push a roof down; it sucks it up, and the force is highest at corners, edges and ridges. Most failures we see after hurricanes start with sheeting fixed with too few screws, purlins tied with light clips or columns anchored for gravity only. We calculate uplift for the site and design every link in the chain: sheeting fixings at closer spacing on edges, purlin connections, frame-to-column joints and anchor bolts in the foundation.

Arcotecho or steel-frame roof?

  • Arcotecho: the curved sheet is its own structure, so it is fast and economical for clear spans over warehouses, courts and workshops. It needs strong edge beams and anchorage.
  • Steel-frame roof: trusses or portal frames carry purlins and sheeting; more flexible in shape, accepts insulated panels, skylights, solar and hanging loads.
  • Architectural canopy: slim cantilevers, hidden drainage and premium finishes for hotel entrances and terraces.
