Greywater Recycling and Rainwater Harvesting for Hotels and Villas

The Riviera Maya gets well over a metre of rain a year, most of it between June and November, and still many areas face water cuts, tanker trucks and rising tariffs. Capturing roof water and recycling greywater from showers and laundry can cover irrigation, toilets and pool top-up, and in many villas a large share of total demand. We design the system with the building: gutters, cisterns, filtration, separate networks and controls.

2026 costs: rainwater harvesting for a villa (10–40 m³ cistern, filtration, pump) $80,000–$450,000 MXN; greywater recycling for a villa $90,000–$400,000 MXN; combined system for a hotel or condo with dual plumbing and treatment $1.5M–$6M MXN. Cheapest when built in from the start.

What We Design and Build

Rainwater Capture

Roof and terrace drainage, first-flush diverters, leaf screens and sizing to rainfall records.

Cisterns

Underground concrete or modular cisterns, waterproofed and sealed against mosquitoes and contamination.

Greywater Recycling

Collection from showers, basins and laundry, filtration and disinfection for safe non-potable reuse.

Dual Plumbing

Separate, colour-coded networks for potable and reused water, with backflow protection.

Irrigation

Drip and subsurface irrigation fed by reused water, with controllers and rain sensors.

Monitoring

Level sensors, automatic switch-over to municipal supply and consumption tracking.

What reuse can realistically cover

Toilets, irrigation, laundry pre-wash, pool top-up and cleaning are the uses that do not need drinking-quality water, and together they are often 40–60% of a villa’s demand and a big share of a hotel’s. Rainwater can also become drinking water with reverse osmosis and UV. Because rain falls mostly in summer and autumn, cistern size is the key decision: we model rainfall and demand month by month so the system does not run dry in the spring dry season.

Build it in, don’t bolt it on

  • Dual plumbing costs little in new construction and a lot in a finished building.
  • Cisterns are best placed during excavation, when the rock is already open.
  • Roof design (slopes, gutters, materials) determines how much clean water you collect.

For hotels, reuse also helps with environmental authorisations and sustainability certifications.
