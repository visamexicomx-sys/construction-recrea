Hotel MEP & Energy in Tulum: Chillers, VRF, Hot Water and Controls

Cooling, hot water and power are the biggest running cost of a hotel in Tulum and its most visible failure; we replace them with the hotel open and cut energy bills on the way. Much of the beach strip still lives on generators, trucked water and private treatment plants, and staff housing is scarce and expensive, so energy, water and housing projects pay back faster here than almost anywhere else.

Tulum, 2026 references: Chiller plant: $4.5M–$28.2M; VRF: $100k–$205k; Heat-pump hot water: $1.7M–$6.8M.

Systems We Replace and Upgrade

Hot Water

Heat pumps and heat recovery replacing diesel or LP boilers.

Electrical & Back-Up

Switchgear, transformers, generators and transfer switches sized for hurricanes.

Chiller Plants

High-efficiency chillers, pumps and cooling towers, swapped in phases without losing cooling.

How the climate in Tulum wears out plant rooms

Salt, sargassum and hurricanes hit the beach strip, while inland plots sit on thin soil over karst and caves, so foundations, wastewater and water supply need more engineering than in Cancún or Playa. We specify coated coils, stainless fixings and ventilated plant rooms so new equipment lasts in these conditions.

Replacement windows in Tulum

High season runs from mid-December to April and around events; many boutique beach hotels close or slow down in September and October, which is the natural window for heavy works. Chillers and boilers are swapped unit by unit in these windows, with rental plant when needed.

Permits and authorities in Tulum

The municipality of Tulum, the National Park and federal environmental authorities all review projects here; many beach hotels sit on land with ZOFEMAT, MIA and land-use issues that must be cleared before any expansion.
