Parking Lot Construction: Paved, Drained and Marked

A parking lot is the first and last thing customers and guests see, and the place where flooding, potholes and faded lines cost reputation every day. We design and build parking lots for hotels, shopping plazas, condominiums, clinics, restaurants and warehouses with the right pavement for each zone, drainage that copes with tropical storms, accessible spaces, lighting conduits and clear markings.

2026 prices: complete asphalt parking lot (earthworks, base, 5 cm asphalt, drainage, striping) $800–$1,350 MXN/m²; concrete parking lot $1,050–$1,650 MXN/m²; permeable or grass pavers $900–$1,600 MXN/m²; line marking $25–$55 MXN/linear m; wheel stops $450–$900 each; solar canopies quoted separately.

What We Deliver

Layout & Design

Space count, aisles, turning radii, accessible bays, loading zones and pedestrian routes to municipal parking rules.

Pavement by Zone

Asphalt for bays, concrete at entrances, ramps, bins and truck routes where loads concentrate.

Storm Drainage

Grades, kerb inlets, trench drains and infiltration wells sized for tropical downpours.

Striping & Signage

Thermoplastic or traffic paint, arrows, accessible symbols, wheel stops and signs.

Lighting & Canopies

Conduits and bases for lighting, CCTV, EV chargers and solar canopies built in from day one.

Landscaping & Shade

Tree pits, planters and shade that keep cars cool and help meet green-area requirements.

Design first: spaces, flow and water

Municipal rules set how many spaces a hotel, shop or clinic needs, their size and how many must be accessible. Good design goes further: one-way aisles that avoid jams at check-in, delivery routes that do not cross pedestrians, and levels that send water to inlets rather than to the entrance. We draw the layout, check it against the regulations and the vehicle turning paths, and only then build it.

Where parking lots go wrong

  • Ponding because grades were never surveyed; water softens the base and potholes follow.
  • One pavement everywhere: thin asphalt at the entrance where trucks and bins turn breaks first.
  • No conduits: adding lighting, cameras or EV chargers later means cutting the new pavement.
  • Cheap paint that fades in one season of sun and rain.
