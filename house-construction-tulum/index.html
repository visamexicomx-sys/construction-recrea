House Construction Process Step by Step

This is how we run every new build, with the timings we actually see in Tulum:

1. Lot review

Land use, legal status, utilities and cenote or cavern risk. Before you buy, if you have not bought yet.

1–2 weeks

2. Concept and budget

Layout, massing and a preliminary cost per m². This is where the real scope is set against your budget.

2–3 weeks

3. Construction documents

Architectural, structural, electrical and plumbing drawings signed by a DRO, plus a closed itemised budget.

4–6 weeks

4. Permits and licences

Land use, building licence, DRO, CFE and water hook-ups, environmental permit where required.

4–20 weeks

5. Foundation

Soil mechanics, rock excavation, footings or mat foundation and under-slab services.

3–5 weeks

6. Shell and structure

Structure, walls, slabs and roofs — the stage that decides how the house holds up against hurricanes and salt air.

10–16 weeks

7. Services and finishes

Electrical, plumbing, air conditioning, plaster, floors, carpentry, ironwork, pool and chukum.

12–20 weeks

8. Turnkey handover

Cleaning, systems testing, house manual, as-built drawings and a written 1-year warranty.

1–2 weeks

Total: 8 to 14 months of construction plus 3–5 months of design and permits.

Permits and Building Rules in Tulum

Tulum is the strictest municipality on the coast. On top of land use and the municipal licence with a DRO, most projects need environmental authorisation from SEMA (and an MIA where there is land clearing or proximity to cenotes or mangrove). The PDU caps density and height by zone and requires a minimum permeable green area. Budget 3 to 5 months for permits.

The lot: what decides your foundation

Fractured limestone with frequent cenotes and caverns, especially in Región 15 and La Veleta. A geophysical survey is not optional here: finding a cavern under the footprint after the foundation has started costs weeks of delay and hundreds of thousands of pesos.

Tulum is the most expensive m² in the region — logistics, environmental rules and a premium finish standard (chukum, hardwood, bioclimatic design) — and also the strongest vacation-rental yield.
