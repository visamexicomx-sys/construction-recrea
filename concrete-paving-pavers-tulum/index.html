Concrete & Pavers in Tulum: concrete, stamped concrete and pavers

Where trucks turn, water sits or the look matters in Tulum, concrete and pavers outlast asphalt and can let rain soak into the ground. There is almost no storm sewer, so water must be infiltrated on site; permeable paving, swales and infiltration wells are the usual design, always located away from cenotes and septic or treatment systems.

Permeable paving and rules in Tulum

Tulum’s urban development plan sets minimum absorption areas on each lot, so hard paving is limited and permeable solutions are often required; clearing jungle needs SEMARNAT authorisation, and near the National Park extra environmental review applies. Permeable pavers, pervious concrete or grass pavers are chosen where these rules and the site make them the right answer.

Bases for concrete and pavers in Tulum

Plots sit on thin soil over porous limestone with caves and cenotes nearby, so cavity checks matter more here than anywhere else on the coast; the beach road zone is sand over rock with a high water table. Base thickness, joints and edge restraints are designed for this ground so slabs and pavers stay level.

Traffic and work windows in Tulum

Rainy-season downpours from June to October stop earthworks for days; the busiest tourist months from December to April slow deliveries on the federal highway, so heavy works are best planned for May and November.

What gets built in Tulum

Tulum is building fast: access roads and streets for new subdivisions in Region 15 and La Veleta, private roads to jungle villas and eco-hotels, car parks near the new airport and the Tren Maya station, and many sascab tracks waiting to be paved.
