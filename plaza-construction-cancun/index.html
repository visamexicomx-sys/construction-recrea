Parking sets your leasable area

Do this before anything else. Retail parking ratios are demanding and restaurants are assessed more heavily than shops, so the leasable area is not what the density allows — it is what the required spaces leave room for, plus circulation, service access and landscaping. Surface parking is the norm for neighbourhood plazas; structured parking rarely pays unless land is very expensive. Visibility and an easy in-and-out turn matter more than area, and delivery and refuse access should be separate from customer parking.

What the landlord builds, and what tenants build

The landlord builds the structure, roof, external walls, storefront openings, floor slab, and services capped at each unit — power with its own meter, water and drainage, sized generously. Tenants build partitions, ceilings, finishes, A/C, glazing and signage to the plaza’s design criteria. Two decisions pay for themselves many times: designate two or three units as food-capable from day one (extraction route to the roof, grease trap, heavier power and water), and write storefront criteria so the plaza does not look improvised within two years.

Building in Cancún: what is different

Cancún is the largest retail market in Quintana Roo and the most competitive: anchors and national chains choose locations here first, and the municipality of Benito Juárez reviews parking, Civil Protection and signage closely. The Hotel Zone and Puerto Cancún add their own development rules on top of the municipal ones, and every project on the coast is designed for hurricane wind loads.
