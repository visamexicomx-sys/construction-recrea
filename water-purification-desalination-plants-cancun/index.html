Reverse Osmosis and Desalination Plants in Cancún

Cancún's municipal water is pumped from wells far inland and, during dry spells and peak season, pressure drops and cuts are common in many neighbourhoods. Large Hotel Zone resorts have long run their own reverse-osmosis or desalination plants for reliability, Puerto Cancún and Tajamar towers add storage and treatment to protect residents, and Costa Mujeres and Isla Mujeres projects often have no other choice. We build those plants and the storage behind them.

Quick answer: In 2026 a seawater desalination plant for 50–500 m³/day costs about $3.5M–$25M MXN including intake, pre-treatment and brine disposal.

Reliability is the real product

For a Cancún resort or condo, the water plant is insurance: when the network pressure drops in April or after a storm, guests and residents still shower. That is why we design with two trains or a standby unit, storage for at least a day of demand, automatic switch-over between network, plant and cistern, and alarms on the operator’s phone. In the Hotel Zone, plant rooms sit close to the sea, so pumps, frames and pipework are stainless or duplex and the room is ventilated and raised above flood level.

Permits and water rights

Wells and beach wells require a CONAGUA concession; brine disposal needs authorisation; large plants may need environmental approval from SEMARNAT or the state. Projects in Costa Mujeres and Isla Mujeres file with their own municipality. We prepare the technical files and the hydraulic tests that support them.
