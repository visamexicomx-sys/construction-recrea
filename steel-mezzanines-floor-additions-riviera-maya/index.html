Steel Mezzanines and Floor Additions on Existing Buildings

Land on Quinta Avenida, in Cancún's commercial strips and in Tulum is expensive; height is often already paid for. A steel mezzanine turns double-height space into extra sales floor, storage or offices, and a light steel addition puts a new floor or rooftop on a building that could not take more concrete. We check the existing structure first, then design, fabricate and install, often while the business stays open.

2026 prices: steel mezzanine inside an existing building with composite deck (losacero) and concrete topping $5,500–$11,000 MXN/m²; light storage mezzanine with steel grating or deck $3,500–$6,500 MXN/m²; rooftop or additional floor in steel frame, shell only $8,000–$14,000 MXN/m². Finishes, stairs and installations are separate.

Can my building take a mezzanine or an extra floor?

It depends on the existing columns, foundations and the original design loads, which in older buildings in the region are often undocumented. We start with a structural review: drawings if they exist, inspection, measurements and, if needed, test pits at the foundations. Often a mezzanine can be carried on its own columns with new footings, independent of the old structure. For additions on top, steel weighs a fraction of concrete, which is what makes many projects possible at all.

Permits and condominium rules

  • Mezzanines and extra floors change the built area, so they need a municipal construction licence with structural calculations and DRO.
  • Land-use limits (height, built area, density) must allow the addition; we check them before design.
  • Condominiums and plazas require the administration's approval and set working hours.
  • Protección Civil reviews exits, fire protection and load signage for commercial use.
