Asphalt Paving Built for Caribbean Heat and Rain

Asphalt in the Riviera Maya suffers more than almost anywhere: surface temperatures above 60 °C soften the binder, trucks leave ruts, and tropical rain finds every weak joint. We lay hot-mix asphalt with paver and rollers on properly prepared bases, use polymer-modified binders where traffic and sun demand them, and measure thickness and density so you get what you paid for.

2026 prices: hot-mix asphalt 5 cm laid on existing base $300–$480 MXN/m²; overlay 4 cm on old asphalt including tack coat $260–$420 MXN/m²; milling $60–$130 MXN/m²; new base + prime + 5 cm asphalt $650–$1,050 MXN/m²; polymer-modified mix adds 10–20%. Minimum mobilisation applies to small areas.

The base matters more than the asphalt

Asphalt is a thin, flexible skin: it lasts only as long as the base under it stays dry and firm. Before paving we check and correct the base (thickness, compaction, levels and crossfall), apply a prime coat on new granular bases or a tack coat on old asphalt, and only then lay the hot mix. Skipping these steps is why so many parking lots and streets in the region crack and pothole after one rainy season.

What makes a good asphalt job

  • Mix delivered hot, from a plant close enough that it arrives at laying temperature.
  • Specified thickness checked with cores, not estimated by eye.
  • Compaction with the right rollers in the right sequence while the mix is still workable.
  • Joints and edges cut, sealed and confined so water and traffic do not unravel them.
  • Drainage with at least 2% crossfall so water runs off instead of soaking in.
