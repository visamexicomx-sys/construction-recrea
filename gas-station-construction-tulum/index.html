Gas Station Construction and Remodelling in Tulum

Service stations — civil works, tanks, canopy and forecourt, with the station trading where possible

A service station in Tulum carries three regulators at once: the federal CRE to operate, ASEA for environment and industrial safety, and the state SEMA that every project in this municipality passes through. We handle the civil side inside that stack rather than beside it.

Three authorities, not two

Fuel retail is regulated nationally: the permit to operate comes from the Comisión Reguladora de Energía, and environmental and industrial safety sit with ASEA, the hydrocarbons regulator. That is the same anywhere in Mexico. What is specific to Tulum is that the state SEMA is also involved before the municipality will open a construction file.

In practice the owner or the brand runs the CRE and ASEA track, we run the construction track, and the SEMA filing has to be sequenced against both. A programme that ignores any one of the three produces a finished station that cannot legally dispense fuel.

Containment where the ground is cenote country

Tulum sits on karst threaded with cenotes and underground conduits. A tank pit here is not a hole in soil: void verification is mandatory before excavation is priced, and containment is designed on the assumption that anything escaping the pit reaches groundwater quickly.

That raises the standard on the impermeable containment, the monitoring wells and the interceptor. It also makes the forecourt falls a pollution question rather than a drainage one — standing fuel-contaminated water is not something this ground forgives. The water table here is mid-depth, which helps, but the conduits do not care about depth.

Services, corrosion and the remodel

Outside the urban area there is no sewer network and CFE capacity is resolved lot by lot, so a treatment plant, water storage and often a capacity increase belong in the first budget. Canopy steel, fixings and island trim are specified for the real distance to the coast, with the maintenance interval handed over in writing.

Most station work is a remodel, and closing costs more than the work. We phase island by island: one side trades while the other is rebuilt, with fuel deliveries and customer routes agreed before the first barrier goes up, and hot works scheduled away from the tank farm.

The ground in Tulum: what it imposes on the project

  • Municipality: Tulum
  • Environmental regime: SEMA; SEMARNAT on the coastal strip
  • Ground: karst under low jungle
  • Water table: mid depth
  • Salt exposure: depends on the sub-zone

The state SEMA sets the calendar; the environmental file is the rule, not the exception.

  • Federal SEMARNAT filing: the environmental resolution is the project’s critical path. Budget 4–8 months before the first excavation.
  • State SEMA authorisation comes before the municipal licence: without it the town hall will not open a file.
  • Karst ground: a soil study with void verification before you price the foundation. A cavity found after the pour costs more than the entire study.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle the CRE, ASEA and SEMA filings?

We run the construction file and the SEMA sequencing, and coordinate with whoever runs CRE and ASEA — usually the brand or the owner’s specialist. What we guarantee is that the works programme matches all three tracks.

How long does a station take in Tulum?

Longer than in Playa del Carmen or inland Cancún, because SEMA is added to the federal track. The honest answer depends on where the CRE and ASEA files stand when we start; the civil works themselves run a few months on a straightforward site.

What does cenote-sensitive containment change?

The specification, not the concept: impermeable containment, monitoring wells and an interceptor sized on the assumption that a leak reaches groundwater fast. It is designed before excavation, not fitted afterwards.

Can the station keep trading during a remodel?

Usually yes, phased island by island, with delivery windows and customer circulation agreed in writing before mobilising.

What if the site has no drainage or CFE?

Normal outside the urban area: treatment plant, water storage and either a capacity increase or own generation. It belongs in the first budget rather than in a change order.
