Cold Storage Warehouse Construction in the Riviera Maya

The Riviera Maya feeds hundreds of thousands of hotel rooms every night. Fish, meat, dairy, fruit, ice cream, beer and medicines all need an unbroken cold chain from the port, the airport and the highway to the kitchen. We build cold storage warehouses, cold rooms and food distribution centres designed for 35°C heat, 80% humidity, power cuts and hurricanes.

2026 costs: cold rooms inside an existing warehouse $9,000–$16,000 MXN/m² of room; new refrigerated warehouse (chilled and frozen zones, docks) $16,000–$28,000 MXN/m²; refrigeration plant, back-up generator and controls priced with the design. Docks with sealed shelters $250,000–$450,000 MXN each.

What We Build

Chilled & Frozen Rooms

Rooms from +4°C to −25°C in insulated panels, with heated floors under freezers to prevent frost heave.

Sealed Docks

Dock levellers with insulated doors and shelters that keep heat and humidity out during loading.

Refrigeration Plant

Systems sized for peak season with redundancy, chosen with your refrigeration contractor or ours.

Back-Up Power

Generators and automatic transfer so a power cut or storm does not cost a full inventory.

Hygiene-Grade Floors & Drains

Food-safe floors, coved joints and drains that pass health inspections.

Heat-Proof Envelope

Reflective roofs, insulated walls and vapour barriers for the Caribbean climate.

Cold chain in a hot, humid, hurricane-prone climate

A cold store in Quintana Roo fights the climate every hour. Outside air at 35°C and 80% humidity means every open door brings in heat and moisture that turns into ice on evaporators and floors. We design vestibules, sealed dock shelters and fast doors, specify continuous vapour barriers, and heat the slab under freezer rooms. Because power cuts happen and hurricanes come every season, back-up generators with automatic transfer are part of the base design, not an option.

Where cold storage makes sense

  • Cancún: food and beverage distribution for the Hotel Zone and Costa Mujeres, near López Portillo and the airport road.
  • Playa del Carmen: supply for Playacar, Mayakoba and the coastal corridor, on the 307 and Avenida Juárez.
  • Tulum: a fast-growing hotel market now served by its own airport and the Tren Maya.
  • Chetumal: regional distribution towards Bacalar, Mahahual and Belize.
